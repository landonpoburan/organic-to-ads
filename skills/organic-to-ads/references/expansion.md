# Expand a selected post

Use when the user asks for expansion, variants, copy, scripts, or related content. Start with a selected post and its review, not a new unrelated campaign. If no review exists, complete accessible source review using [creative-review.md](creative-review.md) and state remaining limits first. Carry forward organic strength and pursuit priority, plus readiness and direction for the chosen ad use. A high-priority post that needs adaptation can proceed into supported draft development; do not interpret unfinished launch inputs as a request to stop all creative work.

## Keep the learning intact

Write a short working brief:

```text
Source post and observed signal:
Goal, audience, awareness, offer, and next step:
Likely mechanism worth preserving:
Proof presented in the source (not independently validated):
Weakness that needs adaptation:
Source facts that must remain accurate:
Available material and production constraints:
Hypothesis: If [audience] sees [message/proof] through [concept],
then [behavior] may improve because [reason].
```

Separate the layers:

- **Angle:** the meaning or interpretation of the problem/desire/offer.
- **Concept:** the creative idea that makes that meaning tangible.
- **Hook:** the opening attention and relevance device inside the concept.
- **Format:** static, carousel, founder video, demo, testimonial, etc.
- **Execution:** the actual copy, images, sequence, speaker, and pacing.

Five synonym swaps are not five concepts. A hook test preserves concept/proof/offer and changes the opening. A discovery batch can vary full concepts; label that several variables change and causality will be less clear.

## Develop branches from the actual source

Use the post's specific structure to choose directions. A useful branch carries forward a reason the source might have worked, rather than only its subject. Examples:

| Source mechanism | Expansion branch | Preserve and test |
|---|---|---|
| A real question or DM creates tension | Expert response video; question-first carousel; FAQ follow-up | Preserve the real question and its honest answer. Test curiosity versus a direct answer. Obtain permission or remove identifying details before reusing a private message. |
| A visible process earns saves | Hands-on demonstration; process carousel; one-frame worked example | Preserve the steps and visible proof. Test showing the result first versus explaining the problem first. |
| A supported statement changes a belief | Statement static; explanation video; objection-led copy | Preserve the evidence and nuance. Test whether the statement can stand alone without losing its support. |
| An actual story earns identification | Speaker-led story; excerpt plus commentary; related email | Preserve the true sequence and speaker. Test the story opening versus the useful takeaway; do not embellish the case. |
| A useful checklist earns repeat reference | Checklist carousel; resource-preview ad; follow-up tutorial | Preserve practical value. A downloadable-resource branch depends on an actual resource and destination. |

Do not expand a source into every branch. Choose those supported by its evidence, the requested outcome, and available production. Separate paid assets from related organic/email/long-form content: each should have its own purpose and next step. The same source can support both, but a viral follow-up and an offer-led ad answer different needs.

For a general expansion request, give a few genuinely different concept cards with draft openings and a recommendation. For a request to write ads or scripts, deliver complete requested drafts, not only ideas. Identify a lower-effort option and a broader concept test when useful, without manufacturing a production estimate.

## Choose the requested expansion

| Output | What to provide |
|---|---|
| Hook variants | Distinct opening mechanics, the source insight each preserves, and honest payoff. |
| Static ads | Headline, supporting line if useful, proof, visual direction, primary text, and CTA. |
| Carousel | Slide-by-slide argument, first-slide promise, proof sequence, caption, and closing next step where relevant to the use. |
| Video | First frame, spoken script, essential visual/demo directions, source proof, and a CTA when relevant to the chosen use. |
| Ad copy | Primary text, headline, CTA, source proof, and what this variation tests. |
| Related organic content | Follow-up posts, FAQ, tutorial, email, or longer piece with a distinct purpose and a clear relationship to the original idea. |

If the user requests general expansion without a count, three useful directions are a starting point. Do not make every asset type by default. Rendered graphics or video require a specific request and available tooling; this skill supplies production directions without a house renderer or paid generation service.

For each concept card, include: source, hypothesis, audience/awareness, angle, concept, format, first frame/hook, core message, source proof, offer bridge and CTA where relevant to the use, production requirements, and the variable being tested. Scale the detail to the requested deliverable.

## Generate and refine the requested drafts

Use audience language from actual captions, comments, transcripts, or a supplied voice guide. Start with a recognizable situation when that suits the source; bridge to the method or technical detail after establishing relevance. Technical openings can work for specialist audiences, so treat this as a choice, not a ban.

Vary opening mechanics meaningfully: a direct demonstration, real question, specific contradiction, story moment, or supported result. A curiosity hook must deliver its promised answer in the asset. Label the difference each variant tests. Generate the requested count; do not force a 25-hook batch or four voice rewrites into a small request.

For primary text, use distinct paths when the source supports them: a true story into the offer; useful explanation into the next step; or audience experience into a practical invitation. Match the immediate commitment to the destination. A free checklist ad should explain the checklist's value; a paid product or consultation needs the relevant decision information. Do not impose high-ticket qualification, financial-status assumptions, or invented urgency on every offer.

For video, provide the first frame, spoken opening, argument/demo beats, proof on screen, and close. For a carousel, make each slide advance the argument. For a static, ensure the selected statement remains accurate outside the source's full context. Do not reuse an organic "link in bio" close for an ad that should direct to a confirmed landing page.

Before delivery, review the drafts against the source:

- Is every specific story detail, quote, and numerical claim traceable to supplied material? A paraphrase must not turn one event into a repeated history or a possibility into certainty.
- Does each direction preserve the named learning and make a distinct test?
- Does the promise match the actual destination and supplied proof?
- Does it sound like the speaker, with specific language and natural rhythm, rather than generic marketing copy?
- Are any weaker-performing patterns from this account being reintroduced?

Revise once where a stronger supported opening or clearer offer bridge is available. Show material sourcing corrections or unresolved dependencies briefly; do not bury the usable drafts under a long self-review. Rank ideas by fit, evidence, and feasibility, explaining why the first choice deserves a test.

## Source and voice

Preserve the advertiser's voice from supplied material; do not impose the author's personal palette, fonts, price point, or target audience. Keep first-person statements faithful to what the actual speaker supplied; do not introduce independent claim validation. Do not fabricate testimonials, named customer stories, screenshots, revenue, transformations, urgency, or guarantees. Drafts may use clearly marked proof placeholders until the advertiser supplies evidence; such drafts are not launch-ready.

Use the actual source to build a natural bridge to the offer. Adapt organic references such as "link in bio" or "comment KEYWORD" to the intended journey when known. If the journey remains unclear, name the missing decision rather than inventing a funnel. Match destination promises to ad promises.

Claim/source validation and Meta policy alignment are outside this skill's scope. Do not certify compliance or add a policy audit to the expansion. Accurate sourcing means not inventing or amplifying content; it does not mean independently proving the source is true.

## Match the CTA to the selected use

For Instagram-profile growth, consider retaining the existing content and directing the ad to the profile; optionally draft a relevant follow prompt. Do not insert a training/sales CTA when that is not the selected goal. For engagement/message-led leads, draft a specific DM keyword/resource invitation only from an actual or clearly proposed resource. Include the response journey and distinguish manual follow-up from existing automation. Do not invent a bot, lead qualification, or promised resource delivery. Attention-focused engagement may need no offer CTA.

## Test recommendation

Choose the smallest useful first test based on the goal and production constraints. Name what stays constant, what changes, the business outcome, and the comparison that would support another iteration. Match the main outcome to the chosen use: viewing/attention may be primary for video-view creative, while qualified messages, leads, or sales are primary for direct response. Do not promise follower growth or downstream conversions from views. Organic multiples are not paid decision thresholds. Use supplied spend limits and real baselines only.

Deliver requested draft artifacts directly. Stop before platform writes or campaign launches unless the user separately requests and authorizes those actions through an appropriate tool.
