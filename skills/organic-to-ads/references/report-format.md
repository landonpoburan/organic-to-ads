# A report that leads to a decision

Lead with the candidate to pursue, its priority, readiness for each recommended ad use, and the next action. A useful opening is: "High-priority organic performer: [post]. Test it as-is for [relevant awareness use]; for [direct-response goal], recommend [small adaptation]. Its [signal] and [source mechanism] support these hypotheses." If nothing merits development, say so and explain the deciding gap.

Priority answers "which post should we pursue?" Readiness answers "what does it need for this particular ad use?" Show organic strength first, then assess the uses. A strong reel may be ready for a video-view test as-is and need a CTA edit for enquiries. Do not collapse those paths into a blanket adaptation verdict or a generic hold.

## Summary first

Start every review with a clearly labeled **Summary** of roughly 100–150 words, shorter for a single post. Use at most five short bullets, combining keep/change when helpful:

- **Recommendation:** selected post/link, organic strength, and pursuit priority.
- **Ad uses:** primary use plus a useful alternative, with readiness and reuse/adaptation direction for each. If no goal is supplied, present options without forcing a primary use.
- **Why:** one or two deciding signals, with comparator count and any material uncertainty.
- **Keep / change:** preserve the source mechanism and name the smallest adjustment needed for each use.
- **Next step:** one concrete action and any dependency that changes the decision.

The summary must stand on its own. Include a material limitation such as partial asset access or provisional metrics here, not only deep in the report. If nothing merits development, state that and the deciding reason. Keep raw metric tables, timestamps, full coverage notes, alternatives, and the test brief below a **Details** heading. Do not repeat the full recommendation as another long opening paragraph. For a simple review, omit detail sections that add no useful evidence.

## Details for an account scan

Use this structure, shortening sections when the request is small:

1. **Recommendation:** candidate to pursue, organic strength, pursuit priority, primary ad use and useful alternative, and deciding reason. Rank a few candidates rather than reproducing every post's metric output. Avoid filling a quota.
2. **Coverage:** account, publication window, posts scanned/analyzed/excluded, measurement ages, assets actually inspected, caps, and unavailable evidence. Distinguish supplied creative descriptions from images/video actually viewed.
3. **Decision table:** show organic evidence and commercial judgment separately.

| Post | Measured signal and confidence | Pursuit priority | Ad use and intended outcome | Readiness, direction, and next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Link or stable label; format | Metric/value vs baseline; multiple; comparator count; observation/reference window; uncertainty | High / Medium / Low / Undetermined, with reason | Awareness/views, audience building, existing warm-audience nurture, Instagram-profile growth, engagement/message-led attention or leads, or enquiries/sales; one row per recommended use | Readiness for this use; reuse, edit, re-record/develop, inspiration, or skip; specify the work |

Show the important component metrics for the priority candidate, including rates where available. Avoid a single blended score or an unsupported probability of success. Include at least one useful weaker comparator when available and explain why a high-reach post was skipped if that affects the recommendation.

Use separate rows for the same post when readiness differs by use. For warm-audience nurture, name the existing audience and explain why the content is useful to people already familiar with the business. A CTA is optional; do not require a lead/sales outcome or claim engagement directly measures trust. A missing CTA does not disqualify awareness/video-view use. Name the smallest CTA edit for direct response, and distinguish a recommended adaptation from a requirement that prevents testing the original. Do not assess factual validity, sources, policy alignment, rights, or music eligibility.

4. **Why this candidate:** use the evidence map from [creative-review.md](creative-review.md). Connect the actual asset's opening, complete argument/demo, supported proof, and close. Identify whether the CTA is in the asset, caption, or both. Label observed facts versus plausible drivers. Cite a slide/timestamp or actual passage. Repeated caption boilerplate is not the video's topic. Do not infer audience demographics or buyer quality from engagement. Explain why the weaker comparator challenges or supports the hypothesis, disclosing any comparator access gap.
5. **Preserve / change / remove:** name the exact source learning and changes. Explain whether the fix is an edit to the existing asset or a re-recording/development of the idea. Do not strip away the mechanism just to fit a format. If an inaccessible passage prevents a definite creative plan, mark only that part provisional and state what resolves it. Preserve source fidelity without adding claim-validation tasks.
6. **First test:** use the practical brief below.
7. **Optional expansion:** offer two or three concrete source-based branches, such as "show the demonstrated process on camera," "answer the objection raised in slide four," or "turn the checklist into a follow-up tutorial." If requested, provide actual drafts following [expansion.md](expansion.md).

## Practical first-test brief

| Field | Decision to make |
|---|---|
| Source and hypothesis | Which post, which mechanism, which audience behavior, and why? |
| Asset and effort | Reuse/edit/record/build; name the work and required material. Explain what makes it low or high effort without inventing production time. |
| First comparison | Current relevant creative if supplied, or a second supported version. If there is no control or performance baseline, call it exploration. |
| Constants | Intended audience, destination, offer, and delivery settings when known. |
| Changed variable | Hook, proof, CTA, or full concept. A format-and-copy change is a concept comparison, not an isolated format test. |
| Destination | Match the use: Instagram profile, product/training page, resource, message flow, or no off-platform destination for a viewing/engagement test. Mark unconfirmed journeys as dependencies. |
| Main outcome | Match the use: relevant engagement/viewing for existing warm-audience nurture; relevant views; profile visits/attributed follows; meaningful engagement; message/resource requests; or qualified enquiries/leads/sales. Define relevance/qualification from supplied criteria. |
| Measurement | For profile growth, assess visits, attributed follows, cost per follow, and visit-to-follow conversion where available. For message-led leads, assess starts, relevant requests, qualification, and cost per relevant result. State missing attribution; account growth alone does not prove ad-driven follows, and likes/comments are not leads. |
| Diagnostic metrics | Metrics serve the chosen objective. Viewing/completion can be primary for a video-view test; clicks and engagement can explain a lead/sales test but do not replace its business outcome. Do not imply views guarantee followers or downstream conversions. |
| Iterate / stop | Compare against real supplied goals or an actual control. Without limits, attribution, or adequate data, state what must be supplied before a launch decision. Do not invent a budget or a universal success bar. |

Missing paid benchmarks or budget inputs should not stop the creative recommendation. Give the proposed asset and an exploratory comparison, then distinguish the implementation inputs needed before launch. A test recommendation should connect the preserved source insight to the business outcome, rather than merely saying "try this post."

Give one first step the advertiser can act on. For example, "replace the organic closing slide with the confirmed product-page CTA" is more useful than "improve conversion." Drafting a test brief does not authorize launching it.

## Before delivering

Can the reader understand the recommendation, reason, main change, next action, and material uncertainty from the short summary alone? Are organic strength and pursuit priority separate from readiness for each ad use? Was accessible full creative actually reviewed? Can they trace the mechanism to the asset rather than boilerplate? Are the baseline and uncertainty visible? Does every expansion preserve a stated source insight? Could someone create the suggested asset without guessing its proof or destination? Remove repetitions and revise vague copy before delivering.
